III. Feedback Opportunities
Formative (Whilst you’re working on the coursework)
In addition to the seminar/lectures that cover the assignment when it is set, you may
contact members of the module team to discuss your approach to the coursework as
required. The first submission of the coursework is for the purposes of formative feedback
only. You will receive feedback from the lab/seminar tutor immediately when presenting
your work as part of the demonstration the week after this first submission.
Formative Feedback Submission (14.30pm 14th November 2025)
Each group should think of itself as a start-up software house developing a solution for a
customer. The customer has allowed discovery meetings to allow you to gather
requirements. Your start-up team has to submit its Software Requirements Specification
document.
You will have access to a scenario document that describes the problem that the customer
needs to be addressed, and the lab tutor will act as a broker in this project who understands
what the customer needs – should you have further questions.
The software requirements specification where the individual requirements conform to the
needs of being unambiguous, testable, clear etc. as will be covered in taught module.
There are no word or page limits, so include all the details you need to. Also note
that being concise whilst delivering the necessary content is a skill. So do not simply
“pad out” your reports with superfluous content: say what you need to clearly and
concisely.
Each group must submit their report in one file (MS WORD) to the group dropbox.
The week after submission during the labs/seminars, all group members will be required to
demonstrate (show and talk through) the content of the report to the lab tutor for
immediate formative feedback.
The report should contain the following sections (only sections 1 to 6 of the coursework
template) and it must include a completed contributions section, this is compulsory and
can be found in Appendix A of the coursework template on NOW.:
1. Introduction
a. Purpose
b. User Characteristics
c. Assumptions
d. Scope and Constraints
e. Glossary of terms
f. Overview
2. Functional Requirements
35
3. Non-Functional requirements
a. Usability requirements
b. Reliability requirements
c. Performance requirements
4. Interfaces
a. User interfaces
b. Hardware interfaces
c. Software interfaces
5. Use Case models
a. Use Cases
b. Misuse cases
6. Project Plan
Appendix A: Contributions
A template for the above structure providing further details of what each section should
contain is available on NOW in the assessment area.
The contributions section will be used by the markers to modify, as required, your individual
grade for the coursework. For example, a low contribution will lead to a lower grade.
Summative (After you’ve submitted the coursework)
You will receive specific written feedback together with your awarded grade when it is
returned to you via NOW Dropbox. Feedback provided with your coursework is for
developmental purposes so that you can improve for the next assessment or subject-related
module.

V. Moderation
The Moderation Process
All assessments are subject to a two-stage moderation process. Firstly, any details related to
the assessment (e.g. clarity of information and the assessment criteria) are considered by an
independent person (usually a member of the module team). Secondly, the grades awarded
are considered by the module team to check for consistency and fairness across the cohort for
the piece of work submitted.

VI. Aspects for Professional Development
Professional Practice
In this module, we have focused on two key learning objectives
which are listed as follows:
 Understand more about the demands of a professional work
context and possible scenarios
 Learn how constraints and requirements affect working
practice (quality, time, cost, commercial considerations,
materials and documentation standards etc.)
The reports cover examples of: Writing a scientific-style report; Researching existing
literature; Referencing appropriately; Construction and proper labelling of figures; Critical
evaluation of methods.
Many of these are useful transferable skills for employment applications
